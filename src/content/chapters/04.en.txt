We'd arranged to meet at half past seven in the evening. I was coming from home and had about an hour's drive, so I stopped off first to buy some slightly gourmet nibbles and a few Complot beers, because if I had to connect with someone, at least let it be over decent food and beer. I set off with the classic second-date nerves: impossible to disguise, but fun too. The kind of nerves that make you feel a bit like a teenager even though you haven't been one for years.

I parked in the spot he'd pointed me to, and this is where my pre-emptive fear turned up without warning. The bag was staying in the car. We'd see whether I felt comfortable enough to stay. And it wasn't just any bag. Inside there were three complete outfits ready to go, my creams, my supplements and absolutely everything I needed. I'd made myself a list. A list! To go to a man's house. Obviously, I had no idea whether I'd be staying one night, three days, or whether I'd be driving home again twenty minutes later, but just in case, I had it all perfectly planned. All I was missing was a folder with the paperwork.

I got to the door thinking of doing one of my "le traigo un paquete" bits, but the flat had a camera and I thought maybe the first thing he saw didn't need to be me clowning around in front of a lens. I buzzed and went up.

It's funny how we behave when we're nervous. I talk a lot, we know that already, but when I'm nervous I can genuinely start treating silences as a personal threat. I don't leave a single gap. And I don't think Max is exactly a specialist in leaving them either, so between the two of us we managed to make the whole thing sound like a live radio broadcast.

He showed me his place, room by room, and then we ended up on the sofa. Beer after beer, a bit of food and conversation. And the hours went by without me noticing. I wasn't bored with Max for a second. He always had some story to tell, some interesting anecdote, something that made me want to know a little bit more.

At some point I noticed he was starting to move closer to me. And I still wanted to talk. I still wanted to analyse a few little things. In fact, I'd prepared a game of eighty-two quick-fire questions. Yes, eighty-two. I don't know whether that says more about me or about my ability to organise a date as if it were a job interview.

I said to him: "Do you want to play a game?" He didn't say no. He stretched out on the sofa and pulled me down to lie beside him. I read the questions off my phone screen, out loud, one by one. And, surprisingly, it seemed we had quite a lot in common: from what we liked to eat to the way we understood relationships. I liked discovering that.

Halfway through the game he kissed me.

And I told him I wanted to finish the questions.

I think that amused him.

When we got to the last one, I put the phone down.

"Now, yes."

And this is where the adventure began.

We headed to the bedroom and it all happened with that mixture of desire, nerves and attraction we already knew. There was a lot of passion, a lot of contact and that feeling of having been holding ourselves back the whole time. On top of that, I was wearing a gorgeous little set I'd put on specially. Which, thinking about it, is slightly absurd, because a man almost never ends up seeing it the way you'd imagined it. But I liked it, and that was enough.

Afterwards we showered and I was already thinking that now, surely, we'd go to sleep. But before getting into bed, Max suggested we go and fetch the bag from the car, since I had things in there. And then, out of nowhere, he made the joke:

"Shall we go on the bike naked?"

I knew perfectly well it was a joke and I played along. He, on the other hand, believed me.

The funny thing is that, honestly, I would have done it. Nobody knows me there.

In the end, the bag stayed in the car and we went back to the bedroom. If I'd already prepared a list of everything I needed, it seems that night I'd also prepared the logistics of a stay I still didn't know would even happen a little too well.

My cough, on the other hand, was quite clear that it intended to stay.

It was that ugly, noisy, persistent cough that seems to have a personality of its own. Max gave me water and cough syrup and tried to look after me as best he could. Sitting up made it a bit better, so we ended up there, in no rush, passing the time.

At one point I gave him a quadriceps massage because he was in pain and I know a bit about physiotherapy. He was very happy about that. Then we started giving each other back massages, with his music playing in the background, and the whole atmosphere kept getting more and more comfortable.

And I was really enjoying myself.

So much so that I got as far as thinking: "Tomorrow I'm telling Ofèlia and Olívia that I'm falling in love if we carry on like this."

I move very fast. I know.

It must have been gone midnight when I noticed Max starting to fall asleep. The music was still playing, the light was still on and I, as always, was keeping an eye on everything.

"Max, don't fall asleep, we've got everything switched on."

"I'm very happy right now, I'm not falling asleep."

And we carried on a while longer.

Until, when he did want to go to sleep, he asked whether I'd mind if he slept in the other room because of my cough.

I think I faked being fine with it very well.

Inside, though, my face was saying: "You're joking, right?"

Because yes, I'd told him about thirty times that we could sleep separately. But it's one thing for you to say it and quite another for a man to say it to you after such a good evening. That you don't see coming.

"Yes, no problem at all," I said, in my kindest voice.

He gave me a little sheet, laid it over me and asked:

"Shall I leave the door ajar?"

"Close it, please."

I needed to plan my escape strategically.

It didn't take me long. I gathered my things, took a breath and thought: this is where I leave you. It wasn't that I was angry. I simply didn't know exactly what he'd understood me staying a few days to mean. He kept telling me he was leaving early the next morning, but did that mean I had to leave too? Or could I stay? I'd never know. What I did know was that I didn't want him to be the one telling me to go.

Rule number one of an empowered woman: when you feel it's the right moment to leave, leave. And don't look back. Never let a man tell you to go before you've decided it yourself.

I knocked on his bedroom door.

"Max, I'm off."

He must have been stunned.

"Violeta, you're not bothering me, you know."

"I know. But I'll be more comfortable sleeping at home."

I picked up my little bag, got dressed and left with my dignity.

And with the bag I'd luckily left in the car.

The hour's drive home flew by. Not because I wasn't tired, but because I spent the whole of it analysing absolutely every minute of the night. I admit it annoyed me to leave. It had been great. I'd had a really good time. But something inside me was telling me I had to do it.

When I got home, I sent him a voice message telling him how good it had all been and letting him know I was back.

Hot one minute, cold the next.

Apparently that's how we hook men.

I slept pretty badly that night, but the next day I woke up calmer. Luckily, Max already understood that my night-time escape had more to do with the cough than with him. And we carried on talking as normal.

But there was one important difference. I'd already made a decision: I liked Max. I don't know if I'd said it that plainly until now, but I'd decided it. And once that decision was made, my job was done. There was nothing else for me to do.

Just see what happened.

And, for the first time, I allowed myself not to run ahead.

I remember it was the day before a long weekend and I was at the gym. I was coming out of the sauna and we were sending each other the odd photo as we left. At one point I thought I could ask him a question. And this is where my own particular battle started up again: the battle between the woman who seems utterly clear and sure of herself and the woman who, faced with a four-word question, is capable of turning a glass of water into the Atlantic.

"Can I ask you something?"

Oh my God. Getting to the point is so hard for me.

He didn't answer and changed the subject on me.

And I, who apparently have everything under control, decided to act as if nothing had happened and went straight to the question I actually wanted to ask.

"What are you doing tomorrow evening?"

My heart was pounding.

That was the question.

Incredible how I can drown in a glass of water.

Max explained his weekend plan to me in full detail: he was going away with some friends to climb and to kitesurf, with the vans. An epic plan. And as I listened to him, I felt that little sting of jealousy and, above all, a bit of frustration at having lost the chance to spend another day with him.

But then I remembered my decision.

I liked Max. And that was that.

There was no need to force anything. Another moment would come.

So I let him be the one to take the next step.

And then he came out with:

"Were you going to suggest something?"

I didn't answer.

I wished him a good trip.

I don't know whether that was mystery, strategy, or simply a fairly personal inability to admit that yes, obviously I'd been about to suggest something.

Over those days we kept sending each other photos. Him from his adventure, surrounded by friends, vans, sport and open air; me with a very different life. And even so, we kept reaching for each other. We didn't need to be having a constant conversation or reporting every move of the day, but there was curiosity. I wanted to know what he was doing and he wanted to know what I was doing.

And maybe that was one of the things starting to fit.

I think I passed on a certain peace and calm to him. And for me, on the other hand, that side of him made up of adventure, of moving around, of having plans, friends and a life that didn't depend on me, was very attractive.

Not because he was the same as me.

Precisely because he wasn't.
