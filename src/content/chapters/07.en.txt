The next day we didn't speak all day.

And, oddly enough, I was very calm.

Very calm. At peace.

I suppose I was starting to understand that what really matters isn't the number of messages a person can send you in a given period of time, but the actions behind those messages. The consistency. That the interest they show has continuity. And with Max, from the very beginning, that was there. Maybe it wasn't a constant conversation and he didn't always reply quickly, but there was reciprocity. There was interest. And, above all, steadiness.

And we'd already been talking for almost a month.

A month.

Which, bearing in mind that we'd met on an app and that at the beginning I used to delete men over much smaller things, is no small matter.

Around eight in the evening on Monday I wrote to him. He was just getting off work and he started telling me things about his job, complaining a bit, filling me in. And I listened to him, thinking about the difference with my life right now. He was immersed in work, the new flat, responsibilities and a thousand stories; I, on the other hand, had a lot more flexibility.

And the truth is I was glad about it.

I'd also finished painting my room and I was very happy. And there was a certain irony here that I couldn't ignore: being thirty years old and absolutely thrilled because I'd made my room at my parents' house look beautiful.

But it was real.

That space was good for me. I felt again that I had a place that was mine.

We carried on talking for a while until, before going to sleep, I remembered something.

"By the way, you have to tell me about that plan you were going to suggest."

The plan he'd promised me.

He explained that he wanted to break in the new flat, but that he still had practically nothing there. Just a bed.

And I thought we didn't need much more than that either.

"With two chairs, a pizza and the bed you already have, I think we've got more than enough."

And that's how it was settled.

But before going to sleep I threw something else at him.

This time it wasn't a joke.

Over the last few times we'd met, I'd been the one doing most of the moving. I'd gone all the way to his place, I'd made suggestions, I'd changed plans… and I didn't mind having done it. I have a lot more flexibility than he does and travelling is no trouble for me.

But adapting is one thing and getting used to always doing it is another.

So I told him, more or less, that I completely understood that right now he had a lot of work, the new flat and everything else, and that I had more flexibility and could adapt. But that I wanted him to know that I liked things to be two-way. Balance. Things going in both directions.

I told him that and I went to sleep.

Utterly calm.

The next day he replied that I was absolutely right. That, at that moment, things were working that way because of the circumstances we were in, but that he was taking it very much on board. And he also told me he appreciated the gestures I was making to come closer to him.

And I really liked the answer.

Not because he'd agreed with me.

Because he'd understood what I was telling him without getting defensive.

Another point for Max.

And me, happy.

From there we carried on talking and reached that very particular phase of starting to share songs, Spotify playlists and all those little things you show someone when you want them to come a bit further into your world.

And that made me think of something curious.

One of the first conversations I'd had with Marcos on WhatsApp had also been us sharing songs with each other. It amused me because, at the beginning, we sent each other music we were really into at that moment, but later, during the relationship, I realised that it didn't say much about his day-to-day taste.

Yes, he listened to those songs.

But they weren't exactly the ones he usually listened to.

Our musical tastes didn't fit together very well either. What for me might be music for a night out, for him could be part of the soundtrack of any ordinary Tuesday.

And there's absolutely nothing wrong with that. I don't think a relationship depends on sharing the same playlist. But it made me think about how, when you get to know someone, you keep discovering little things that maybe seem insignificant and that, all of a sudden, make you wonder whether that person fits with you in more areas than you'd imagined.

With Max, for the time being, the music also looked promising.

Let's see if we fit here too.

I was starting to be quite curious to find out.
